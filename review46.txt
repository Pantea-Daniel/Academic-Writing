Review of “Experimental Comparison of Sorting Algorithms Using Different Input Patterns”

The paper is in the scope of the assignment. It studies sorting algorithms, experimental benchmarking, complexity analysis, and the influence of different input structures on practical performance. The topic fits well within the objectives of the scientific paper assignment.

This paper presents an experimental comparison of several classical sorting algorithms implemented and benchmarked in Python. The study includes Bubble Sort, Selection Sort, Insertion Sort, Merge Sort, Quick Sort, Heap Sort, Counting Sort, and Python’s built-in Timsort implementation. The algorithms are tested on multiple input sizes and on several input patterns, including random, sorted, reverse sorted, almost sorted, half sorted, and flat lists. The paper combines theoretical complexity discussion with practical execution measurements and graphical visualization of the results.

The presentation of the paper is clear and easy to follow. The structure is logical and follows a standard academic organization with introduction, theoretical background, methodology, experiments, discussion, limitations, conclusions, and references. The language is simple and appropriate for a university assignment, and the explanations are understandable even for readers with limited prior experience.

The main results are reasonably important for the level of the assignment. The experiments successfully demonstrate the practical differences between quadratic sorting algorithms and O(n log n) methods. The paper also shows how input structure affects practical performance, especially for Insertion Sort and Timsort. The inclusion of very large input sizes, up to 10,000,000 elements, gives the work additional practical value.

The results are reasonably non-trivial to achieve. The author implemented multiple algorithms, generated several categories of test data, benchmarked different input sizes, collected timing results, and created graphical comparisons. Organizing experiments with multiple input patterns also improves the quality of the analysis compared to simpler benchmark studies.

The results seem to be original in the context of this assignment. The sorting algorithms themselves are classical, but the paper combines multiple algorithms, multiple input structures, and large-scale benchmarks into a coherent practical study. The paper also clearly distinguishes between theoretical expectations and experimental observations.

The paper contains enough information to understand and partially verify the correctness of the work. The theoretical complexity section is correct overall, the benchmark setup is explained sufficiently, and the conclusions are generally consistent with standard algorithm theory. The graphs on pages 5–9 are also useful for visualizing the scaling behavior of the algorithms and the influence of different input patterns.

From a technical perspective, the author seems to understand the subject sufficiently well. The complexity discussions are mostly correct, and the interpretation of the benchmark results is reasonable. I have partially checked the technical explanations and did not notice major conceptual errors. The discussion about why some algorithms were excluded from the largest benchmarks is also sensible and academically honest.

However, there are several areas where the paper could be improved. The methodology section remains relatively brief, and more precise experimental details would strengthen the work. For example, the paper does not specify the hardware configuration, Python version, operating system details, or exact timing methodology used. Some graphs are difficult to read because many algorithms are displayed together with overlapping lines and labels. In addition, the paper is sometimes more descriptive than analytical. The results are explained correctly, but the analysis could go deeper into why certain algorithms behave differently in practice, especially regarding memory usage, recursion overhead, cache effects, or implementation overhead in Python.

The presentation quality is generally good for a student paper. The figures are relevant, the references are appropriate, and the writing is readable. The related work section is also better integrated than in many similar assignments because it compares similarities and differences with prior studies instead of only listing sources. However, there are still some minor language and formatting issues, and a few sections repeat ideas already mentioned earlier in the paper.

For improvement, I would suggest expanding the experimental methodology section with more hardware and software details. The paper could also include exact measured values in tables in addition to the graphs. Statistical information such as averages over multiple runs, variance, or standard deviation would improve the rigor of the benchmark results. Some graphs could also be simplified for readability. Finally, the discussion section could include a deeper explanation of implementation-level performance differences between pure Python algorithms and optimized built-in functions.

Overall, this is a solid and well-organized university project. The paper demonstrates a good understanding of sorting algorithms, complexity analysis, and practical benchmarking. The experiments are meaningful, the input-pattern analysis is useful, and the conclusions are consistent with both theory and observed results. While the work remains mostly educational rather than highly original or deeply technical, it is technically sound and appropriate for the assignment requirements.

Final recommendation: Weak Accept.
